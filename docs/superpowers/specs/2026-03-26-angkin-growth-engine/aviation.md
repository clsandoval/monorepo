## Hub Page
- **URL:** /aviation/
- **H1:** "Aviation Calculators & Tools — Philippines"
- **Overview:**

The Civil Aviation Authority of the Philippines (CAAP) is the sole regulatory authority for all civil aviation in the country, governing approximately 8,000+ licensed pilots, 3,000-5,000 Aircraft Maintenance Technicians (AMTs), 2,000+ Air Traffic Controllers (ATCs), 500-1,000 registered civil aircraft, and a rapidly growing commercial drone sector estimated at 2,000-5,000 operators expanding at 15-20% annually. CAAP administers three primary compliance tracks: personnel licensing (the pilot pathway from Student Pilot License through Private, Commercial, to Airline Transport Pilot License), aircraft airworthiness and registration (Certificate of Registration and annual Certificate of Airworthiness), and RPAS/drone compliance (a 3-certificate stack for commercial drone operators introduced under PCAR Part 11).

The angkin.ph Aviation hub provides free tools that decode CAAP's regulatory framework across these three tracks. Philippine Civil Aviation Regulations (PCAR) Parts 2, 4, and 11 define exact flight hour minimums, exam subjects, fee schedules, and eligibility gates — making significant portions computationally tractable. The pilot licensing pathway involves 4 sequential tiers (SPL, PPL, CPL, ATPL) with progressively higher flight hour requirements (40 hours for PPL, 150 for CPL, 1,500 for ATPL), each gated by CAAP Airmen Examination Board written exams and practical tests. For the drone industry, CAAP's three-certificate requirement (Controller Certificate, Operator Certificate, Registration Certificate) catches many commercial operators by surprise, with penalties of PHP 20,000-100,000 per violation for non-compliance.

While CAAP aviation markets are smaller than mass-market regulatory domains (SSS, LTO, PhilHealth), the commercial drone sector is the highest-growth segment with fully deterministic compliance rules, high penalty exposure, and zero existing public compliance tools. The pilot licensing pathway complements this with strong SEO search volume from aspiring aviators.

- **Hub FAQs:**

1. **How much does it cost to become a pilot in the Philippines?** CAAP government fees for the full CPL pathway from scratch are approximately PHP 9,000-12,000 (exam fees + license issuance). However, flight school training costs dominate: PHP 3-5 million for a full CPL program. CAAP exam fees are: pre-solo theory PHP 230/subject, PPL theory PHP 230/subject (10 subjects = PHP 2,300), CPL theory PHP 340/subject (9 subjects = PHP 3,060), ATPL theory PHP 450/subject (9 subjects = PHP 4,050), per CAAP MC 20-15.

2. **Do I need a CAAP license to fly a drone commercially?** Yes. Commercial drone operators need up to three CAAP certifications: (1) RPAS Controller Certificate (PHP 3,360, valid 5 years), (2) RPA Registration Certificate (PHP 1,680/year), and (3) RPAS Operator Certificate (for businesses, valid 3 years). Even for recreational drones weighing 7 kg or more, registration is required.

3. **What are the penalties for flying a drone without CAAP certification?** Operating a drone without registration carries a penalty of PHP 20,000-100,000 per violation under PCAR Part 11. Operating without a controller certificate incurs additional penalties. Enforcement is increasing as the commercial drone sector grows.

4. **What flight hours do I need for each pilot license level?** Per PCAR Part 2: SPL to PPL requires minimum 40 hours total flight time (20 solo, 5 solo cross-country). PPL to CPL requires minimum 150 hours total (100 PIC, 20 instrument, 20 cross-country). CPL to ATPL requires minimum 1,500 hours total (500 multi-engine, 100 night, with Class 1 medical).

5. **How often do I renew my Certificate of Airworthiness?** CofA is valid for 12 months maximum. Renewal requires a CAAP inspection within 30 days of expiry. Late renewal results in suspension of CAAP services and flight plan rejection. Fees are MTOW-based across 4 weight classes (per CAAP MC 018-2023).

6. **What CAAP medical certificate do I need?** Class 2 medical is required for SPL and PPL. Class 1 medical is required for CPL and ATPL. Medical exam must be conducted by CAAP-designated Aviation Medical Examiners. Medical class requirements are a common expensive discovery for aspiring commercial pilots.
