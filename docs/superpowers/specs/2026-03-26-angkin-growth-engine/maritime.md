- **URL:** /maritime/

- **Overview:**

The Philippines deploys 621,484 sea-based OFWs annually (2023 revised DMW figure) — the world's largest seafarer supplier, providing roughly 30% of the global merchant fleet's officers. The Maritime Industry Authority (MARINA) governs the certification of approximately 1,000,000 registered seafarers through a complex STCW Convention compliance framework involving Certificates of Competency (COC), Certificates of Proficiency (COP), mandatory STCW training courses (BST, ATFF, PSCRB, GMDSS, ECDIS), and 5-year revalidation cycles. In 2025 alone, MARINA processed 583,642 total STCW certificates including 520,759 COPs, 39,397 COCs, and 23,486 GMDSS certificates.

The angkin.ph Maritime hub provides free calculators that decode MARINA's 622-line Omnibus Fee Schedule (MC GC-2026-01), the STCW Convention's sea service eligibility tables (A-II/1, A-II/2, A-III/1, A-III/2), and the multi-certificate revalidation process that every Filipino seafarer must navigate every 5 years. Currently, no official MARINA tool or third-party application covers the full certification pathway, eligibility calculation, or cost estimation — seafarers rely on Facebook groups (500K+ combined membership in Pinoy Seafarer communities), manning agency HR staff, and fragmented MISMO portal information.

The stakes are high: a seafarer who misses a COC revalidation deadline cannot deploy, losing USD 500-1,500 per delayed deployment. MISMO application rejections for incorrect documents cause weeks-long delays. Maritime school graduates entering the profession face PHP 15,000-30,000 in initial STCW certification costs with no official cost calculator. The 2024 Magna Carta of Filipino Seafarers (RA 12021) further reshapes the landscape by granting MARINA first-time authority to regulate training center fees. For domestic vessel operators, the Annual Tonnage Fee (ATF) adds fleet compliance obligations with 25% late penalties.

- **Hub FAQs:**

1. **How much does STCW certification cost for a new seafarer?** Total out-of-pocket for a new seafarer seeking a Certificate of Competency (COC) is approximately PHP 15,000-30,000. This includes MARINA document fees (SRB PHP 1,000 + SID PHP 600 + COC PHP 530 = PHP 2,130) plus mandatory STCW training courses (BST PHP 3,500-8,000, ATFF PHP 3,500-6,000, PSCRB PHP 3,500-8,000, MEFA PHP 2,500-5,500). Prices vary across 87+ MARINA-accredited training institutions.

2. **How long is a COC valid, and how do I revalidate?** COCs are valid for 5 years from date of issuance (STCW Regulation I/11). Revalidation requires: (1) proof of recent sea service (3 months in the last 5 years for operational level, 12 months for management level), OR completion of an approved refresher course if sea service is insufficient; (2) all COPs still valid (not expired beyond 5 years); (3) updated PEME medical certificate from a DOH-accredited clinic; (4) MARINA MISMO online application.

3. **What sea service do I need to qualify for OIC-NW (Officer in Charge of Navigational Watch)?** Under STCW Table A-II/1, you need either: Option A — 12 months structured onboard cadet training on ships 500 GT or above, OR Option B — 36 months of deck department service on ships 500 GT or above. Both options require completion of mandatory training courses (BST, PSCRB, ATFF, GMDSS, ECDIS, MLC).

4. **How much did MARINA reduce COC fees?** MARINA reduced COC fees in April 2022 from PHP 1,000 to PHP 530 (regular) and from PHP 1,500 to PHP 780 (expedited) per MC SC-2022-02, pursuant to the Ease of Doing Business Act (RA 11032). COP fees are PHP 200 regular, PHP 600 expedited.

5. **What is the MARINA Omnibus Fee Schedule (MC GC-2026-01)?** The 2026 Omnibus Fee Schedule consolidates all MARINA fees into a single reference with 622 line items. Approved by the MARINA Board on November 20, 2025, it covers COC/COP fees, SRB/SID fees, vessel registration, training institution accreditation, and Annual Tonnage Fees. Most seafarer-related fees (42 items) remain unchanged from previous rates.

6. **Do I need to pay for STCW training out of pocket?** For contracted seafarers, manning agencies often cover STCW training costs as part of pre-deployment processing (charged to the shipowner, per RA 12021). Individual seafarers between contracts and new entrants typically bear costs themselves. Training costs range from PHP 3,500-8,000 per mandatory course at MARINA-accredited institutions.

7. **What changes does RA 12021 (Magna Carta of Filipino Seafarers) bring?** RA 12021, signed in 2024, gives MARINA first-time authority to set maximum and minimum STCW training fees — meaning training center costs (currently opaque and varying widely) will become regulated once implementing rules are issued. It also strengthens minimum wage provisions for Filipino seafarers.

8. **What is the Annual Tonnage Fee and who pays it?** The ATF is an annual fee payable by owners/operators of Philippine-registered domestic trade vessels (excluding fishing and non-motorized vessels below 3 GT). It is computed based on total gross tonnage of the fleet per MC 2008-05/2008-07, updated by MC GC-2026-01. Late payment incurs a 25% surcharge plus 1% per month per MC 120.
