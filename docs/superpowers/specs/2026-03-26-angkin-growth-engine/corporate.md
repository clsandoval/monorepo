## Hub Page

- **URL:** /corporate/
- **H1:** "Corporate & SEC Compliance Calculators & Tools -- Philippines"
- **Overview:**

Every Philippine corporation registered with the Securities and Exchange Commission faces a recurring compliance burden spanning annual filings (GIS, AFS), penalty exposure for late or missing reports, beneficial ownership disclosure under the new HARBOR system, capital structure changes, and occupational safety staffing requirements. With 527,710 active SEC-registered corporations at year-end 2024, 52,304 new registrations that year alone, and 117,885 corporations suspended in a single February 2024 batch order, the compliance universe is massive -- and the penalties for non-compliance are severe.

angkin.ph's Corporate & SEC Compliance hub provides six free calculator tools that replace the guesswork and professional intermediary fees (P36,000-P300,000/year corporate secretary retainers; P50,000-P200,000+ per remediation engagement) with instant, statute-grounded computation. From determining your corporation's compliance status and computing exact penalties under MC-6-2024, to tracing beneficial ownership through multi-layered corporate structures for HARBOR filing, to verifying whether your corporation actually needs a full audit or qualifies for reviewed financial statements under the new P3M threshold -- every tool cites the specific governing law and produces results you can verify against the statute yourself.

The SEC's own eFAST, eSPARC, and HARBOR portals are filing submission systems -- none of them tell you what you owe, whether you're compliant, or what your cheapest path back to good standing is. These tools fill that gap.

- **Hub FAQs:**

1. **How do I check if my corporation is compliant with the SEC?** Your compliance status depends on your filing history over the past 5 years. Use the SEC Compliance Navigator (E1) to input your corporation type, unfiled reports by year, and equity bracket -- the tool classifies you as good standing, non-compliant, delinquent, suspended, or at risk of revocation per RA 11232 Sec. 177 and SEC MC No. 19 (2023).

2. **How much are the penalties for late SEC filing of GIS or AFS?** Penalties follow the tiered matrix in SEC MC No. 6, Series of 2024, which varies by entity type (domestic stock, domestic non-stock, foreign stock, foreign non-stock), retained earnings bracket, and offense number (1st through 5th, with 100% surcharge on 6th+ offense). A domestic stock corporation with retained earnings over P1M that misses its 3rd GIS filing can face P27,000-P45,000 in fines per unfiled report, plus monthly surcharges of P500-P1,000.

3. **What is the ECIP and can my corporation still avail of it?** The Enhanced Compliance Incentive Plan (SEC MC No. 13, Series of 2024) offered non-compliant corporations a flat P20,000 settlement to resolve all unassessed penalties, and suspended/revoked corporations a 50% reduction on assessed fines plus a P3,060 petition fee. The ECIP deadline was extended twice to December 31, 2024. The SEC has signaled future compliance windows may be offered. Use the SEC Compliance Navigator to compare your full penalty exposure against ECIP-equivalent settlement amounts.

4. **What is HARBOR and when do I need to file?** HARBOR (Hierarchical and Applicable Relations and Beneficial Ownership Registry) is the SEC's new beneficial ownership disclosure system, launched January 30, 2026 under SEC MC No. 15, Series of 2025. ALL SEC-registered stock and non-stock corporations, OPCs, and partnerships must file through harbor.sec.gov.ph. Beneficial ownership data has been removed from the GIS -- it is now a separate filing. Penalties range from P50,000-P1,000,000 for non-disclosure, and P1,000,000 plus 5-year officer disqualification for false declarations.

5. **Does my corporation need audited or reviewed financial statements?** Under the new threshold effective December 31, 2025, corporations with total assets or liabilities exceeding P3,000,000 must submit full PFRS-audited financial statements. Below that threshold, PFRS for SMEs reviewed financial statements are acceptable. This threshold was raised from the previous P600,000 -- meaning thousands of small corporations that previously needed full audits (costing P30,000-P300,000+) may now qualify for reviewed FS (costing P15,000-P50,000). Use the AFS Filing Threshold tool (E4) to check.

6. **How much does it cost to incorporate a corporation with the SEC?** SEC filing fees are computed as 1/5 of 1% (0.2%) of authorized capital stock (ACS) or subscription price, whichever is higher, with a minimum of P2,000. Add the Legal Research Fee (1% of filing fee, minimum P10), By-Laws registration (P1,010), and Documentary Stamp Tax (P1.00 per P200 par value of subscribed shares). For a P1,000,000 ACS corporation: total SEC fees are approximately P3,310. For P10,000,000 ACS: approximately P32,700. Use the SEC Lifecycle Fee Engine (E2) for an exact breakdown.

7. **How many safety officers does my company need under DOLE?** Under RA 11058 and DOLE DO 252-25 (effective May 16, 2025), every workplace must have at least one trained safety officer (SO1). Establishments with 10+ workers and medium/high risk classifications require escalating levels (SO2, SO3) and additional occupational health personnel. Non-compliance penalties range from P20,000-P100,000/day. Use the OSH Staffing tool (E6) to determine exact requirements based on your employee count and risk classification.

8. **What happens if my corporation is suspended by the SEC?** Suspension means your corporation cannot transact -- banks will flag your account, government contracts are blocked, and property transfers cannot proceed. To lift suspension, you must file all back reports via eFAST, settle penalties under MC-6-2024 (or any available amnesty program), and file a petition for reinstatement with a P3,060 fee. The SEC Compliance Navigator computes your total remediation cost and generates a prioritized filing checklist.

- **How It Works Content:**
  1. **Direct ownership test**: If a natural person owns 25%+ of a corporation's voting shares directly, they are a beneficial owner under MC-15-2025. The tool identifies all shareholders meeting or exceeding this threshold.
  2. **Indirect ownership tracing** (the core computation): For juridical entity shareholders, the tool traces ownership recursively through corporate layers by multiplying percentages at each level. Example: Person A owns 60% of Corp B, which owns 50% of Corp C -- A's indirect ownership in Corp C = 60% x 50% = 30%, which exceeds the 25% threshold, making A a beneficial owner of Corp C. The tool handles unlimited layers and circular holdings.
  3. **"Control through other means" flagging**: Even without 25% ownership, a person exercising effective control (through voting agreements, board appointment rights, management contracts) is a beneficial owner. The tool cannot make this determination automatically but flags scenarios where no natural person meets the 25% threshold and prompts users to identify the senior managing official (who becomes the deemed beneficial owner).
  4. **HARBOR-compliant output generation**: For each identified beneficial owner, the tool prepares the required disclosure data: full name, nationality, residential address, nature and extent of beneficial interest (percentage and type of control), and date when they became a beneficial owner.
  5. **7-day change monitoring alerts**: Under MC-15-2025, any change in beneficial ownership must be reported through HARBOR within 7 calendar days. The tool computes whether a proposed share transfer would cross the 25% threshold for any person and flags required HARBOR update filings.

  4. **Capital decrease computation**: Same amendment fee formula (0.2% of the decrease, minimum P3,000). Capital decrease requires showing that the reduction does not prejudice creditors and that the corporation has sufficient surplus. No DST applies on capital decrease (no new share issuance).
  5. **Post-amendment requirements**: Updated GIS must be filed within 30 days; BIR registration update may be required if the capital change affects the corporation's tax profile; bank certificate required for cash subscriptions proving deposit.
