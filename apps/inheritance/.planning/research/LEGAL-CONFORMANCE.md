## 1. Verdict

**No. This engine's legal output cannot be relied on by a practising lawyer today, in either half of the product.** Nine independently reproduced defects are critical: the intestate order cannot route succession to grandparents or any ascendant above a parent (the estate escheats to the State or the widow takes 100%); collateral succession through predeceased siblings emits duplicate heir rows and loses up to 40% of the estate; a total repudiation by all children escheats the estate while living grandchildren receive zero; a predeceased *parent* is credited a full share by an impossible ascending-line representation; preterition annuls legacies the Code expressly preserves and fires on heirs who received advances on their legitime; any estate with a substantial donation *inter vivos* produces per-heir totals that exceed the entire estate (₱30M distributed from a ₱10M estate); Art. 992's iron curtain is not implemented at all, so 100% of an illegitimate decedent's estate goes to a barred marital half-sibling; the TRAIN-repealed ₱500,000 medical deduction is still granted; and the bridge into the succession engine feeds *net taxable estate minus tax* as the distributable estate, understating every heir's share by ~75%.

**Every one of these fails silently.** `warnings` is hardcoded to an empty array at `engine/src/step10_finalize.rs:619`, so no ManualFlag the pipeline builds ever reaches output; and `from_legitime`/`from_free_portion`/`legitime_fraction` are hard-zeroed at `step10_finalize.rs:538-542`, verified zero on **0 of 512** per-heir rows across all 130 committed examples — so a reviewing lawyer cannot even tell which pesos are protected legitime.

**What *is* trustworthy:** the legitime fraction table for descendants, spouse and illegitimate children (Arts. 888–903) conforms exactly, row by row, at every arity tested; direct-line representation gets all four vacancy causes right (predecease/repudiation/incapacity/disinheritance — the classic conflation bug is *not* present); the full/half-blood 2:1 ratio (Art. 1006) and the flat TRAIN 6% rate, ₱5M standard deduction, ₱10M family-home cap, spouse-share ordering and vanishing-deduction sliding scale all conform. The arithmetic core is sound; the failures are in **heir selection, vacancy handling, and the boundaries between modules**.

**Priority order for a fix:** `engine/src/step2_lines.rs:70` is the single highest-value line in the codebase — one hardcoded `degree_from_decedent == 1` anchor filter is the root cause of four separate critical defects. Then `step10_finalize.rs:619` (turn the warnings back on), then the two pipeline boundaries (`tax-bridge.ts`, the preterition/donation branches in `step7_distribute.rs`).

### 2c. Not wrong numbers, but they block any sign-off

These are not law contradictions. They are the reason none of the above is visible to a reviewer, and they should be fixed **first** because every other fix is unverifiable without them.

- **All warnings and manual-review flags are discarded.** `engine/src/step10_finalize.rs:619` hardcodes `warnings: vec![]`. Step 6 demonstrably *builds* a ManualFlag on preterition (`step6_validation.rs:218`); it never reaches output. Only 6 of the spec's 10 flag categories are even constructed. **Every one of 29 findings above reproduced with `warnings: []`.** The spec's entire §13.1 human-in-the-loop mechanism is unreachable.
- **The legitime/free-portion split is thrown away at the serialization boundary.** `engine/src/step10_finalize.rs:538-542` and `:561-565`: `from_legitime: Money::new(0), // TODO: round sub-components`, plus `from_free_portion`, `from_intestate` and `legitime_fraction: String::new()`. Step 7 populates them correctly at `step7_distribute.rs:261-262`. Measured across all 130 committed examples, 512 per-heir rows: **nonzero `from_legitime` on 0 rows, nonzero `from_free_portion` on 0 rows, non-empty `legitime_fraction` on 0 rows.** [Arts. 904 and 905][ra386] attach different consequences to legitime pesos (cannot be burdened, conditioned or waived in advance) than to free-portion pesos, so a lawyer receiving a bare `total` of ₱125,000,000 cannot advise on whether a will condition on it is void.
- **No conservation invariant is enforced at runtime.** `step10_finalize.rs:8-9` documents "Sum invariant MUST hold" and `allocate_with_rounding` (`:311-318`) carries the same post-condition, but nothing asserts it on real output, and no check rejects duplicate `heir_id`s in `per_heir_shares`. ## 3. Lawyer review agenda

Eight interpretive choices the engine has already made, on which it needs a lawyer's signature. **None of these is a bug** — each is a defensible reading, several are the mainstream one. What is missing is a recorded decision. Each is answerable in one sitting; the engine's current position is stated so the answer can be "confirm" or "change to B."

**Q1. One legitimate child + surviving spouse, intestate — is the split ½/½, or ½ to the child and ¼ to the spouse?**
Reading A (engine): [Art. 996][ra386pdf] — the spouse takes "the same share as that of each of the children," so the lone child and the spouse each take one share: **½/½**. This is *Santillon v. Miranda*, G.R. No. L-19281 (1965).
Reading B: apply the testate legitime table — child ½ ([Art. 888][ra386]), spouse ¼ ([Art. 892 ¶1][ra386]), remainder intestate.
Engine implements: **A** — 1 LC + spouse, E=₱12M → ₱6,000,000 each, scenario I2, basis `["Art. 996"]`.
Why it matters: this is the single most common intestate family shape in the Philippines. Everything downstream inherits it.
**What I need:** confirm *Santillon* is still how you would advise, and that no post-1965 decision has unsettled it.

**Q2. One legitimate child + spouse + illegitimate children, testate — is the spouse's legitime ¼ or equal to the child's ½?**
Reading A (engine): [Art. 892 ¶1][ra386] controls — "If only one legitimate child... survives, the widow or widower shall be entitled to **one-fourth**."
Reading B: [Art. 897][ra386]'s literal text — where the spouse survives with "legitimate children or descendants, and acknowledged natural children," the spouse takes "a portion **equal to the legitime of each of the legitimate children**," i.e. ½ where there is one child.
Engine implements: **A** (¼), following the prevailing doctrinal table. The spec cites Art. 897 only for the multi-child case (T5b), so the choice is unrecorded.
**What I need:** confirm Art. 897 is read as addressing the plural case only, and I will write that into the spec with your name on it.

**Q3. When nephews and nieces alone survive and inherit per capita under Art. 975 ¶2, does the Art. 1006 full/half-blood 2:1 ratio still apply?**
Reading A: [Art. 975 ¶2][ra386pdf] — "if they alone survive, they shall inherit **in equal portions**" — flat per capita, ratio gone.
Reading B: [Art. 1008][ra386pdf] — children of half-blood siblings succeed "in accordance with the rules laid down for brothers and sisters of the full blood," which imports Art. 1006's doubling. Tolentino and Jurado are cited for this reading.
Engine implements: **neither** — the per-capita branch is unreachable dead code (see §2a), so the engine currently does per stirpes regardless. The spec flags the debate at `specs/inheritance-engine-spec.md:1400-1412` and proposes a config flag.
**What I need:** pick A or B as the default before the dead branch is enabled, otherwise the fix will hardcode an unreviewed answer. A config flag defaulting to your choice is fine.

**Q4. How far does *Aquino v. Aquino* reach — does the iron curtain still bar a nonmarital claimant in the collateral line?**
Reading A (narrow, and what the Court said about itself): [*Aquino*][aquino] limited its own holding — "this ruling will only apply when the nonmarital child has a right of representation to their parent's share in her grandparent's legitime. **It is silent on collateral relatives** where the nonmarital child may inherit by themself." So [Art. 992][ra386] survives intact for collaterals, and *Diaz v. IAC* remains operative there.
Reading B (broad): the Court's equal-protection reasoning about birth status cannot coherently stop at the direct line, so lower courts should extend it.
Engine implements: **neither as a rule** — it implements no Art. 992 barrier at all, which happens to match A in the direct line and contradicts A in the collateral line (see §2a critical). A nonmarital nephew currently inherits from a marital uncle, and a marital half-sibling takes 100% of an illegitimate decedent's estate.
**What I need:** confirm A so the collateral barrier can be implemented, and tell me whether you want a `LAWYER_REVIEW` flag on every case where the barrier is decisive rather than a silent computation. This is the highest-stakes item on this list.

**Q5. When a compulsory heir's legitime is impaired, should the engine self-execute the reduction, or report it as a claim the heir must assert?**
Reading A (engine): compute the corrected partition directly. [Art. 907][ra386] reduction is applied and the output presents the result as if it were the will's own disposition.
Reading B: Art. 907 reduction happens "**on petition of the same**," and [Art. 906][ra386] gives the heir a right to "**demand**" completion — neither is self-executing. The output should show the will's figures, the legitime, the shortfall, and who must petition.
Engine implements: **A**, silently. Verified: ₱30M, 2 children each given 1/10 with a stranger given 8/10 → engine returns the corrected ₱250,000,000 / ₱250,000,000 / ₱500,000,000 (centavos) with `warnings: []` and a narrative that never mentions that the stranger's 80% bequest was cut by ₱3,000,000 or that lc1's own gift was increased. The disappointed voluntary heir has no notice of the fact he would litigate.
**What I need:** decide whether the deliverable is a computed partition or a computed partition **plus** a disclosure of the procedural posture. This is a product decision with legal consequences and I should not make it.

**Q6. Where an heir's entitlement exceeds the property actually in the estate because of a donation *inter vivos*, should the output show one number or two?**
Reading A (engine, and wrong as implemented): one number, drawn from the collated base — which is how the engine ends up distributing ₱30,000,000 from a ₱10,000,000 estate.
Reading B: two distinct outputs — payable-from-estate, and a *reduction/return claim against a named donee*. [Art. 771][ra386] is explicit that reduction "shall not prevent the donations from taking effect during the life of the donor, nor shall it bar the donee from appropriating the fruits," so the heir gets an *accion de reduccion*, not estate property. Only compulsory heirs may sue, and only for the excess.
Engine implements: **A**. The fix in §2a requires choosing B's data model.
**What I need:** confirm that modelling the heir's remedy as a claim against the donee (rather than as estate pesos) is the right shape, since it changes the output schema.

**Q7. Family home deduction on a conjugal home — ½ of FMV, or full FMV?**
Reading A (engine): `min(fmv × 0.5, ₱10,000,000)`, i.e. the decedent's interest. [RR 12-2018 §7.2.3][rr12]: "the current fair market value... **or the extent of the decedent's interest (whether conjugal/community or exclusive property), whichever is lower**, but not exceeding P10,000,000."
Reading B: full FMV up to the cap. The engine's own spec hedges at ~line 1009 ("Some commentary uses full FMV for conjugal").
Engine implements: **A** — conjugal ₱12M home → ₱6,000,000 deduction; exclusive ₱12M → ₱10,000,000 (capped). I read §7.2.3 as settling this in the engine's favour and would delete the spec hedge.
**What I need:** one line confirming A, so the hedge can go and a regression test can pin it. ₱6,000,000 of deduction turns on it (≈₱360,000 of tax).

**Q8. Does RA 11642 Sec. 41 apply retroactively to adoption decrees issued before 2022?**
Reading A: yes — [RA 11642 Sec. 41][ra11642] extends legitimate filiation to "the adopter's parents, adopter's legitimate siblings, and legitimate descendants," and Sec. 43 gives reciprocal succession "without distinction from legitimate filiations." Applied to a pre-2022 decree, this reverses [*Sayson v. CA*][sayson] ("The relationship created by the adoption is between only the adopting parents and the adopted child and does not extend to the blood relatives of either party"), letting an adoptee represent the adopter in the adopter's parent's estate.
Reading B: no — succession rights vest under the law at the decedent's death, and Sec. 41 is prospective as to decrees.
Engine implements: **neither.** `config.retroactive_ra_11642` and `AdoptionRegime` exist and are **provably dead** — I ran the identical tree as `{Ra8552, retroactive: false}` and `{Ra11642, retroactive: true}` and `diff` of the two complete outputs is **empty**. The engine silently computes every case under the pre-2022 *Sayson* rule.
**What I need:** your answer becomes the default for a flag that currently does nothing. If you would rather not decide, say so and I will make the engine refuse to compute Sec. 41 fact patterns instead of guessing.

- **[Art. 891][ra386] reserva troncal** — *unrunnable by construction*. `EngineInput` has no asset inventory or provenance fields, so no JSON exists that would make the engine behave differently. The negative was confirmed by grep (0 matches in 15,676 lines) and by the absence of any reserva flag among the six flag categories the crate constructs.
- **Spec §7.4 / Art. 992 pre-*Aquino* framing** — a documentation defect with no implementation to exercise. The adjacent numeric evidence (the engine reaching the post-*Aquino* number by omission) *was* run.
- **Spec §6 T12 / Art. 900 ¶2** — spec-only; its downstream numeric effect is demonstrated by the code finding in §2a.
- **RA 11642 Sec. 41's substantive pathway** was not tested for correctness, only for existence — the A/B `diff` proves the flag is inert, but no case was constructed in which an adoptee claims through the adopter's parent, because the engine cannot seat one.

**Not audited at all.** RA 9858 (legitimation) and RA 9255 were checked only for their effect on the legitime ratio and on `Relationship::LegitimatedChild` mapping, not for the full range of who qualifies as a compulsory heir. [Arts. 1012–1014][ra386pdf] (escheat procedure, municipal assignment, the 5-year reclaim window) are out of scope for a share-computation engine and their absence is not treated as a defect. No frontend UI flow was audited beyond the three input fields named in §2a. No test was written; every reproduction was a one-off run against hand-built JSON.

**Where a negative result was accepted as currency.** Every "still in force" claim about Arts. 854, 891, 909–912, 915–923, 960–1014 and 1061–1077 rests on the absence of an amendment annotation on the lawphil page plus targeted searches that surfaced nothing. That is not a confirmation from an official consolidated source. The most recent authority directly verified is [*Morales v. Olondriz* (2016)][morales], with [*Trinidad v. Trinidad*, G.R. No. 254695 (Dec. 6, 2023)][morales] surfaced in search as applying it — the latter was not fetched.
