<objective>
Build `frontend/src/lib/estate-tax-engine/penalties.ts` — the single implementation of everything
Phase 20 knows — plus its unit suite. Nothing is wired into the pipeline by this plan; that is `20-04`.

The module answers three questions and refuses a fourth:

1. **When was this return due?** `specs/estate-tax-engine-spec.md` §21 states the deadline: one year
   from the date of death for a TRAIN-era death, six months for a pre-TRAIN death. That is a
   transcription of a spec sentence, so the module may compute it.
2. **How late is it?** A day count between the statutory deadline and the entered filing date.
3. **What does the lateness cost?** **It declines to say.** The surcharge under NIRC Sec. 248 and the
   interest under NIRC Sec. 249 each become a line carrying its section, `centavos: null`,
   `status: 'declined'`, a reason naming the recorded question, and the id of that question. The
   compromise penalty becomes a third declined line whose authority is the spec's own out-of-scope
   declaration, because no schedule for it is stated anywhere in this repository.
4. **What is the total?** `null`, whenever any line is declined — and the exact integer sum of the
   base tax plus three determined lines, whenever none is. Both branches are implemented and tested
   now, so `20-04` wires a finished rule and Phase 21 exports a total that is either right or absent.

The reason the money lines are declined rather than computed is recorded in `20-RESEARCH.md` §0 and is
not a matter of effort: `specs/estate-tax-engine-spec.md` §1 lists *"Compute surcharges, interest, or
penalties for late filing"* under **What the engine does NOT do**, and §2 lists *"Surcharges,
interest, compromise penalties"* under **Out of Scope**. `.planning/NEW-LEGAL-RULE.md` Step 1 makes
writing a statement no spec contains a point of law, and `CLAUDE.md` invariant 6 forbids any agent
from deciding one.
</objective>

<threat_model>
This module is pure: it reads two strings and an integer and returns an object. It opens no socket,
touches no database, reads no credential and has no side effect. It is reachable from the estate-tax
compute path only after `20-04` wires it.

The real hazard is **semantic, and it is the one the whole phase exists to close**: a future agent
under time pressure fills `centavos: null` with a number because a consumer needed one. Three
defences are built in here rather than bolted on later. First, `centavos` is typed `number | null` so
`strict` mode forces every reader to handle the absence instead of silently coercing. Second, every
declined line carries a `lawyerDecision` id, so the number cannot appear without the registry entry
being answered first. Third, the module contains no arithmetic that could host a rate — no `%`, no
`Math.round`, and a numeric-literal set small enough for gate `G36` to assert exactly.

A second hazard is a **false-confidence deadline**: an off-by-one in the month arithmetic. Its blast
radius is bounded to a day count on screen, because no money is derived from the deadline in this
phase, and `specs/estate-tax-engine-spec.md` §21 itself labels the filing rules *"informational only
— no Form 1801 computation items are affected"*. It is nevertheless pinned by two committed worked
examples asserted as vectors.

Severity: low for the code, high for the precedent — which is why the literal-set constraint is a
constraint and not a preference.
</threat_model>

`statutoryFilingDeadline(dateOfDeath: string): string | null` — returns the ISO date on which the
return was due, or `null` for an empty or malformed input. Algorithm, fixed:

- Trim the input. If it does not match the shape `YYYY-MM-DD` with four digits, a hyphen, two digits,
  a hyphen and two digits, return `null`.
- Read the deadline period from `getDeductionRules(trimmed)`: a `'TRAIN'` result selects
  `DEADLINE_MONTHS_TRAIN`, a `'PRE_TRAIN'` result selects `DEADLINE_MONTHS_PRE_TRAIN`. The boundary

  the resulting year-and-month with `new Date(Date.UTC(targetYear, targetMonthIndex + 1, 0))` and take
  its UTC date. If the original day exceeds that last day, use the last day instead — the month-end
  clamp. This reproduces the two committed worked examples in
  `loops/reverse/estate-tax/analysis/filing-rules.md` lines 261 and 263: a `2020-06-15` death is due
  `2021-06-15`, and a `2015-03-31` death is due `2015-09-30` rather than rolling into October.
- Rebuild the ISO string with `padStart(2, '0')` on the month and day.
