<objective>
`17-RESEARCH.md` section 1 measured the shape of the defect: `HeirNarrative`
(`engine/src/types.rs:583-588`) carries `heir_id`, `heir_name`, `heir_category_label` and `text` and
**nothing else**. `generate_heir_narrative` already builds a per-section `legal_basis`
(`step10_finalize.rs:507` clones `share.legal_basis` onto the CATEGORY section), and
`assemble_narrative` then throws all of it away by joining `s.text` only. So the prose is the only
citation the frontend ever receives from the narrative — and the prose derives its own article.

Measured consequence, over 652 heir rows in 171 corpus files: **615 rows (94.3%) carry a narrative
article absent from that row's `legal_basis`**, dominated by 564 occurrences of `Art. 887` against
table rows that say `Art. 996`, `Art. 980` or `Art. 999`.

This plan makes the engine the single authority in the only way that is structurally sound:

1. **`HeirNarrative` gains `legal_basis: Vec<String>`**, populated by cloning
   `share.legal_basis` at the one site that already has the share in hand. A clone cannot disagree
   with its source. This is what "the table, the narrative, the citation pill and the PDF all read
   that value" means concretely.
2. **The prose stops stating an article.** Eight literal strings lose their citation, and each
   citation is preserved verbatim as a comment on the line above.

The comment technique is not cosmetic and it is not a way around a gate — it is the measured
requirement. `scripts/check-legal-traceability.mjs` recomputes `implemented_in` for every article on
every run by scanning each file's production region, comments included, for `/Art\. (\d+)/`. Removing
the eight prose citations with no compensating change was simulated in `17-RESEARCH.md` section 6 and
produces:

```
Art. 176 ["src/step10_finalize.rs"] -> []   REGISTERED ARTICLE ABSENT
Art. 179 ["src/step10_finalize.rs"] -> []   REGISTERED ARTICLE ABSENT
Art. 887 [step10, step1_classify] -> [step1_classify]   IMPLEMENTED_IN DRIFTED
Art. 972 [step10, step2_lines, step7] -> [step2_lines, step7]   IMPLEMENTED_IN DRIFTED
```

Two Family Code articles would fall out of the engine's legal traceability entirely. With the
comments in place, the same simulation prints **`G28 producedBy deltas: 0`** — the registry needs no
edit at all, and `engine/legal-rules.json` is deliberately **not** in this plan's `files_modified`.

The eight edits, given as exact before-and-after literals in Tasks 3 and 4, are the whole of the
prose change. Task 5 proves the corpus number moved from 615 to 0.
</objective>

<threat_model>
The engine runs as WASM inside the lawyer's browser and receives a family graph and an estate value.
This plan changes what the engine *says*, not what it *computes*, so the classic hazards — a wrong
peso figure, an over-distribution, a lost heir — are out of reach of every edit here. `cargo test`'s
existing centavo-sum assertions are the net that proves it, and they run after each of three tasks.

The real hazard is the opposite of a leak: **a citation silently disappearing**. If an article
literal is dropped rather than moved to a comment, the engine stops declaring where a rule is
implemented, `engine/legal-rules.json` becomes a registry of articles nothing cites, and a later agent
diffing code against the spec re-implements a rule that already exists. G28 is precisely the detector
for that, which is why this plan runs it as a per-task gate rather than only at the end, and why the
registry is excluded from `files_modified` so that "fix G28 by editing the registry" is not available
as a shortcut.

A second hazard is the WASM artifact drifting from the Rust source. The frontend loads the built
binary; a plan that edits Rust and forgets to rebuild ships a narrative that still cites Art. 887
while the source says otherwise, and every later verification in this phase would measure the old
binary. Task 5 rebuilds and then re-measures through the rebuilt artifact, in that order, so a
stale binary cannot produce a green result.
</threat_model>
