<objective>
Make the property corpus able to reach the three heir shapes that break the engine, and record the
breakage rather than hiding it or fixing it.

Measured starting point, from `06-RESEARCH.md` sections 2.2 and 2.3, taken over all 140 committed
inputs: `NephewNiece` appears in **0** files, `recipient_is_stranger: true` appears in **0** files,
the maximum donation/estate ratio is **0.5524**, and five of the eleven `Relationship` variants
(`LegitimatedChild`, `LegitimateAscendant`, `NephewNiece`, `OtherCollateral`, `Stranger`) appear
nowhere at all. `.planning/research/LEGAL-CONFORMANCE.md` line 76 states the same three numbers and
concludes the corpus sits "below the threshold where either breach mechanism can fire". This plan
inverts that.

Two directories, because the shapes split cleanly into two groups that were each measured
individually:

- `engine/examples/coverage-cases/` — thirty generated cases whose outputs conserve the estate and
  produce no duplicate heir rows today. Plan 06-02 makes the invariant tests read them alongside
  `fuzz-cases/`.
- `engine/examples/defect-cases/` — exactly three hand-written cases that violate sum conservation
  today. Each is an already-documented defect owned by a later phase, and each is recorded in a
  shrink-only ledger that fails the build both when the defect gets worse and when it is fixed
  without deleting its ledger entry.

The existing `engine/examples/generate-fuzz-cases.py` is **not edited**. It has a fixed
`SEED = 20260224` and was verified during planning to regenerate all 100 committed files
byte-for-byte; inserting a generator function into it would shift the random stream and rewrite the
whole corpus. A second generator with its own seed writing to its own directory produces zero churn.
</objective>
