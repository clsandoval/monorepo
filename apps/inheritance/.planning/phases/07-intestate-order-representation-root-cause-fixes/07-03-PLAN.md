<objective>
Make a total repudiation by the nearest descendant degree pass the estate to the next degree in its
own right, closing LAW-03.

Measured on 2026-07-31: three legitimate children all with `has_renounced: true`, each with one
living child at degree 2, estate 12000000000 centavos, produces

    scenario I15
    STATE=12000000000 basis=["Art. 1011"] | lc1=0 | lc2=0 | lc3=0
    warnings 0

Three living grandchildren are absent from the output while the State takes the whole estate. Art.
969 says the opposite: "If the inheritance should be repudiated by the nearest relative, should there
be one only, or by all the nearest relatives called by law to succeed, should there be several, those
of the following degree shall inherit in their own right and cannot represent the person or persons
repudiating the inheritance."

Two things currently prevent it. `engine/src/step2_lines.rs` anchors descendants at degree 1 only, so
three repudiating children yield three extinct lines and the grandchildren are never anchored.
`engine/src/step9_vacancy.rs:526` `check_total_renunciation` pools by `effective_category` with no
degree scoping, so the group it inspects contains the living non-repudiating grandchildren and
`all_renounced` is never true — its restart never fires.

The fix is the anchor rule this phase has already applied to the ascendant tier in plan 07-01 and to
the collateral tier in plan 07-02, now applied to the two descendant categories: the anchor set is the
heirs of that category at the **lowest degree at which at least one heir yields a line**, whether that
line is own-right or representation. Three repudiating children yield zero lines at degree 1, so the
rule moves to degree 2, where three living grandchildren yield three own-right lines. That is exactly
"those of the following degree shall inherit in their own right", and it is produced without a
pipeline restart.

Two behaviours must be left exactly as they are, and both are unit-tested below. An ordinary family
with living children keeps degree 1 as its anchor degree, so grandchildren are never anchored. A
family whose children have all **predeceased** keeps degree 1 as its anchor degree too, because a
predeceased child with living descendants yields a `Representation` line — so that family still
passes per stirpes under Arts. 970 and 974, which `.planning/research/LEGAL-CONFORMANCE.md` §6 records
as already correct.
</objective>
