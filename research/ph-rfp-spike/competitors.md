# Competitive Landscape — PH Government RFP Discovery

*All claims below rest on a four-mode adversarial sweep re-verified 8 Aug 2026. Inline tags: **[V]** = independently verified this sweep, **[D]** = modes disagreed, **[U]** = unverified/asserted. Treat **[U]** as a to-do, not a fact.*

## 1. Is this taken?

**No — but it is not empty either, and the empty part is not the software.** Exactly one genuine PH-local product exists: **GEPSearch** (gepsearch.pro), roughly three months old, indexing mPhilGEPS same-day, showing ABC in pesos and procuring entity on the row — the only player in the entire set that does both **[V]**. Everything else serving Philippine notices is a foreign scraping mill (TenderImpulse, TendersInfo, GlobalTenders, PhilippinesTenders, BidDetail, OpenOpps), and several of them are *bigger* than GEPSearch on raw volume — TenderImpulse states 6,586 live PH tenders vs GEPSearch's 3,935 **[V]**. So the honest verdict is: the *discovery* problem is solved badly by many, well by one, and monetized by nobody locally. The unclaimed ground is not "scrape PhilGEPS" — that is demonstrably a solved commodity — it is (a) LGU-first structure and geography, (b) alerting on channels Filipinos actually use, (c) eligibility and award-history intelligence, and (d) peso pricing with local rails. Add one timing fact: **on 1 Aug 2026 — eight days ago — PhilGEPS 1.5 stopped accepting new postings for the largest tranche of government (all Executive departments, their regional/provincial/district offices, OP and OVP), per PS-DBM #PSADVISORY 2026-19 and Advisory 2026-09 [V]**. Every incumbent crawler is currently either re-pointed, half-pointed, or quietly decaying, and none of them will tell you which.

## 3. The graveyard

Five PH procurement products and ~25 repos are dead. The causes sort cleanly, and the sorting is the finding.

**A. Killed by strategic abandonment — the most important death.**
**First Circle "Project Finder"** (my.firstcircle.ph/project-finder) was free, PH-local, PhilGEPS **plus scraped LGU websites**, showing project name, ABC, location, procuring entity, closing date, with daily and weekly keyword email alerts. It was almost exactly the right product. It now renders one sentence: *"We've upgraded our platform! Our old platform is no longer available."* **[V, triple-confirmed across three modes, in a real browser]**. The lender's homepage contains zero matches for "philgeps" or "project-finder"; the product nav is banking and lending only **[V]**. Cause of death: **it was a free lead-gen side-feature of a fintech, never a business, so it died as collateral damage of a platform migration.** The tell: the SEO funnel is *still live* — `/blog/government-bids-project-finder`, `/blog/how-to-bid-in-philgeps`, `/blog/philgeps-requirements` all return 200 today **[V]**. Demand is still being generated and dropped on the floor every day. Its standalone LGU-website scraping died with it and **no verified player has replaced that surface**.

**B. Killed by volunteer/grant infrastructure rot.**
- **philgeps.bettergov.ph** — BetterGov's dedicated PhilGEPS awards explorer (~5M contracts claimed **[U]**, repo real, MIT) returns **HTTP 530, Cloudflare Error 1016 "Origin DNS error"**; `/api/health` is also 530 **[V]**. Sibling subdomains (saln, taxdirectory) are healthy, so this is a specific dead service. **BetterGov still ships the link in production nav** — `{label:"Philgeps", href:"https://philgeps.bettergov.ph"}` **[V]**. Cause: no SLA, no funding, volunteer bandwidth spread across dozens of repos.
- **OCDex (LAYERTech, IRI/USAID-supported)** — the only source that ever deliberately sliced PhilGEPS **by LGU** (South Cotabato, Albay, Legazpi City, Legazpi City Water District, DPWH-Albay). Site up, every dataset's newest last-modified date is **11/4/2019**, zero datasets from 2020 onward **[V]**. Cause: **hand curation does not survive the grant cycle.** It also proves LGU-level demand was real enough that someone curated it manually.
- **WeSolve Foundation** — shipped a real downloadable COVID-contracts dataset, frozen at an August 2020 cutoff, published 2021, never updated **[V]**. Same failure mode: project-cycle dependent.

**C. Killed by "built the extractor, never operated the service" — the dominant pattern, and it is measured, not asserted.**
- `chloebellee/philgeps-scraper` — the cleanest datapoint in the landscape. Full commit log: 7 commits total, six on 3–4 June 2026 building it, then a **64-day gap**, then one lone "chore: update scraped results 2026-08-07". A Mon–Fri GitHub Actions cron that has fired once in nine weeks **[V]**.
- `Niraven/philgeps-scraper` — technically the most sophisticated (Scrapling + Camoufox, ASP.NET postback handling, structured JSON for alerting). Lifespan: **2 hours 11 minutes** from creation to last push, then silence for three months **[V]**.
- `sanesecurityguy/philgeps-bid-opportunities-streamer` — Google Apps Script → Slack, hourly. **The only pure alerting product ever built for PhilGEPS.** Created and abandoned the same day, 22 Aug 2023 **[V]**.
- `josephdlmd/bidscrapperv2` — actually shipped a consumer product at bidindex.vercel.app. Repo lived 17 days; the deployment now **404s** **[V]**. Someone tried the exact commercial idea and it is gone.
- Others, timed to the second: `mikkogozalo` 3m45s (2015), `LanzLagman` 3m40s for 18MB of notebooks, `tltanhueco1990` 13m04s, `Shoebn/gecaws` 26m, `brentdeguzman` 1h34m, `AiRA-9184` same day **[V]**.

**D. Killed before birth — pure intent.** Five artifacts named for the purpose and entirely empty: `bettergovph/open-philgeps-data` (0 KB), `ps-arjay/PhilGEPSOpenData` (0 KB), both `Cris032293/Philgeps-RSS` and `/Philgeps` (0 KB each — the only RSS attempt ever, no code committed), and the HuggingFace `bettergovph/open-philgeps` dataset (EmptyDatasetError) **[V]**.

**E. The original.** `CoreProc/procurement-web` — ProcEx, a real Laravel product by an actual PH dev agency, self-labelled **`[INACTIVE]`** in its own live description; `procex.coreproc.ph` is NXDOMAIN. 5 stars in twelve years **[V]**.

**So what actually kills these — plainly:**
- **Not data access.** This is the single most important correction to the received wisdom. The portal is hostile (ASP.NET WebForms postbacks, Cloudflare, no filterable notice API, deep links returning "Transaction cannot be completed at this time" **[V]**) — but hostile is not closed. TenderImpulse holds 6,586 live PH tenders, TendersInfo indexes to barangay level with same-day dates, GlobalTenders maps rows to verified mPhilGEPS refs, and GEPSearch achieves same-day indexing as a ~3-month-old startup **[all V]**. Extraction is a commodity, not a moat.
- **Overwhelmingly abandonment.** Solo builders ship the scraper — the interesting 20% — and never run the boring 80%: scheduling, dedupe, retries, delivery, and re-pointing when the portal moves under them. The median PhilGEPS repo dies within hours of its first push.
- **Secondarily, it was never anyone's business.** First Circle: free funnel. BetterGov, OCDex, WeSolve: volunteer or grant-funded. None had revenue to defend.
- **Demand risk is real but weakly evidenced.** The one paid product (Apify actor) has 2 monthly active users **[V]** — but its channel is developers, not contractors, and it has no UI. **No PH-local player has ever tested willingness-to-pay:** First Circle was free, GEPSearch's pricing is login-walled. "There is no demand" is *unproven*, not established. The counter-evidence to bear: a lender who could have monetized these leads downstream via financing still chose to kill the product.

## 5. The wedge

**Be clear about what is not a wedge.** Not scraping — six foreign mills and one three-month-old startup already do it, some at 1.7× GEPSearch's volume. Not the search UI — GEPSearch shipped filters for Classification, Mode, Rule, Fund, Category, Budget and Dates in about a quarter. Not "PhilGEPS has no API, so ingestion is a moat" — that framing is **refuted**: `open.philgeps.gov.ph` is live, synced 07 Aug 2026, states it shares "raw data in a JSON, CSV, or XML Format using PhilGEPS API," and `api.philgeps.gov.ph` returns unauthenticated JSON **[V]**. The honest version: *a public registry/analytics API exists; filterable access to live tender notices via API is unconfirmed* **[U]**. Either way, ingestion is a chore, not a moat.

**Wedge 1 — Operational reliability through the migration, sold as a promise.** The 1 Aug 2026 cutover is eight days old **[V]**. The government's own homepage still links bidders to a legacy open-opportunity search that throws an error **[V]**. OpenOpps' largest PH source domain is the legacy host **[V, but the decay inference is a dated hypothesis — re-test in 2–4 weeks]**. Every foreign mill is a black box: none of them will tell a Filipino contractor whether their feed is complete this week. A product that publishes its own coverage audit — notices ingested per day, gap versus the official board, last sync timestamp — converts the one thing incumbents structurally cannot offer (verifiable completeness) into the purchase reason. **Caveat: this is a head start, not a moat.** It decays in months as competitors re-point. Its real value is that it is the cheapest possible reason for someone to switch *right now*.

**Wedge 2 — LGU as a first-class entity, not a string.** This is the most defensible gap and the one closest to the reader's stated emphasis. Every foreign aggregator drops the procuring entity entirely or collapses it to "Philippines"/"Government Of Philippines" **[V]**; none surface ABC **[V]**. GEPSearch has both fields but no LGU geography as a primary axis, and gates the source link behind login. Meanwhile: the only product that ever scraped **LGU websites outside PhilGEPS** (First Circle) is dead with no replacement **[V]**; the only LGU-sliced dataset ever published (OCDex) froze in 2019 **[V]**; PhilGEPS itself admits Sagip Saka and agri-fishery opportunities post to *other* platforms **[V]**. Building a normalized registry of ~1,600 LGUs — canonical names, aliases, province/region hierarchy, PSGC codes, plus district engineering offices — and joining every notice to it is genuinely tedious work that nobody has done and that compounds. It is the substrate for geo alerts ("everything within 50 km"), for LGU-level award history, and for the off-PhilGEPS sources. **The moat here is normalization labour and coverage breadth, not code.**

**Wedge 3 — Current-year award intelligence joined to open notices.** BetterGov's procurement browser states its own coverage is **2000–2025 [V]** — there is no free source of 2026 awards. Apify's schema proves awardee and award amount are extractable today **[V]**. The unbuilt product is one sentence: *"For this notice's agency, here are the last 12 months of awards — who won, at what percentage of ABC, and how many bidders showed up."* That is what GovWin and GovSpend actually sell in the US, it is what a contractor deciding whether to spend three days on a bid actually needs, and it is invisible in every player checked. Add PCAB licence class matching on top and you answer "which of today's bids am I even allowed to bid on" — **no product does this [U: absence not exhaustively proven, but no counterexample surfaced across four sweeps]**.

**The blunt part.** The software gap is small and shrinking; GEPSearch will close most of the discovery layer without you. The data gap is not a moat either — extraction is commodity. What is actually scarce, in order: **(1) distribution** — the PH content cluster owns the search traffic and ships no tool, GEPSearch owns the tool and has no audience beyond a Facebook page, and Buildeee owns exactly the contractor/merchant audience with a Filipino locale and zero bid product **[all V]**; **(2) operational persistence** — the graveyard's dominant cause of death is not denied access but nobody running the cron on day 65; **(3) proven willingness-to-pay, which does not exist yet in this market at any price.** The strongest bear case is a single sentence: a lender with downstream financing revenue to capture still killed the free version. Before writing much code, go find twenty contractors and establish what they would pay in pesos — that number, not the scraper, is the actual unknown.
