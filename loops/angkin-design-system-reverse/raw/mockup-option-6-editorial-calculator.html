Every employed Filipino who has worked for at least five years with the same employer has a right that many don't know they have: a mandatory retirement benefit, guaranteed by the State. Republic Act 7641 — the Retirement Pay Law — was signed in 1992, but three decades later, many workers still leave their jobs without collecting what the law promises them.

The formula is deceptively simple: one-half month's salary for every year of service, with a fraction of at least six months counted as one full year. But "one-half month's salary" is not what most people think it is. It includes more than just your basic pay.

"The law does not ask your employer for a favor. It mandates a minimum. Your employer may give more — but never less."

Republic Act No. 7641

As Amended

Section 1. In the absence of a retirement plan or agreement providing for retirement benefits of employees in the establishment, an employee upon reaching the age of sixty (60) years or more, but not beyond sixty-five (65) years which is hereby declared the compulsory retirement age, who has served at least five (5) years in the said establishment, may retire and shall be entitled to retirement pay equivalent to at least one-half (½) month salary for every year of service, a fraction of at least six (6) months being considered as one whole year.

Source: RA 7641 § 1 · DOLE Advisory No. 06 Series of 2020

What "One-Half Month's Salary" Actually Means

Under DOLE's implementing rules, "one-half month salary" for purposes of RA 7641 is computed as: 15 days of basic salary plus 1/12 of the 13th month pay plus 1/12 of cash equivalents of unused service incentive leaves. This is commonly abbreviated as 22.5 days of basic pay — a number that surprises most workers.

If your company has a CBA (collective bargaining agreement) or a company retirement plan that provides at least equal retirement benefits, that plan governs. If the company plan provides less than what RA 7641 mandates, RA 7641 prevails. You are always entitled to the higher of the two.

Who Qualifies — And Who Does Not

The law covers all regular employees in the private sector who are at least 60 years old and have rendered at least five continuous years of service to the same employer. "Continuous" here has nuance: brief interruptions (illness, authorized leave) generally do not break continuity, but significant gaps might. When in doubt, DOLE's NLRC regional arbitration branches accept clarification requests.

Not covered: government employees (they have GSIS), household helpers (kasambahays have their own law under RA 10361), and employees of retail/service establishments regularly employing not more than ten workers, unless a CBA or company policy applies. Overseas Filipino Workers employed abroad are governed by their host country's law and POEA contracts — RA 7641 applies to Philippine-based employment.

"Rodel had worked 19 years. His employer offered ₱45,000. The calculator said ₱186,000. He printed both numbers and walked back into HR."

What To Do If Your Employer Refuses

File a complaint with the nearest DOLE Regional Office. Bring: your employment contract or appointment letter, your most recent payslips (or any proof of salary), and any documentation of your tenure (IDs, pay records, 13th month pay stubs). DOLE mediation under Single Entry Approach (SEnA) is free and typically resolves within 30 days. If mediation fails, the case proceeds to NLRC arbitration.

The prescriptive period for money claims under the Labor Code is three years from the date the claim became due. Do not wait.
